Keiji Muto debuted for New Japan in 1984 and had his first U.S. runs in Florida and World Class. In WCW in 1989 he became the Great Muta, with green mist and a moonsault far ahead of its time, and he feuded with Sting and Ric Flair.

Back in Japan he was one of New Japan's "Three Musketeers" with Masahiro Chono and Shinya Hashimoto, a multi-time IWGP Heavyweight champion and G1 Climax winner, and he worked as both himself and Muta.

He ran All Japan as its president from 2002 to 2013, founded Wrestle-1, finished his career in Pro Wrestling Noah, and retired in 2023 at the Tokyo Dome.
